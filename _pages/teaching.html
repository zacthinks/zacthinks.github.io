Computer-Assisted Text Analysis for Social Research

Harvard Graduate School of Education · Instructor
Fall 2026

A graduate methods course on using computational tools to study text systematically without giving up the responsibilities of reading, interpretation, validation, and judgment.

The course treats computer-assisted text analysis as a methodological workflow rather than a sequence of disconnected algorithms. Students learn to organize textual corpora, construct and compare representations, explore and retrieve cases, develop and validate measures, use supervised learning, work with imperfect measurements, conduct computer-assisted qualitative inquiry, and evaluate large language models as analytic tools.

The course is organized around the idea of text analysis as purposeful translation. Across the semester, students repeatedly ask what a computational translation affords, how it can be validated, what information it adds, what structure it imposes, and what it loses. Students use TeAL throughout the course and GeCo for computer-assisted qualitative inquiry, while weekly memo-based assignments build toward a final reproducible analysis.
