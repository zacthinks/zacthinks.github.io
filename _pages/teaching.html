S-062: Computer-Assisted Text Analysis for Social Research

A graduate methods course on using computational tools to study text systematically without giving up the responsibilities of reading, interpretation, validation, and judgment.

COURSE DESIGN

Learn the methods by using them.

S-062 treats computer-assisted text analysis as a methodological workflow rather than a sequence of disconnected algorithms. The course is organized around a recurring idea: computational methods translate text into different forms, and those translations should be chosen and evaluated according to the research purposes they serve.

Students use TeAL throughout the semester to build traceable analyses and later work with GeCo for computer-assisted qualitative inquiry. Most units pair short conceptual preparation with a Tuesday theory session and a Thursday application session. Weekly memo-style assignments accumulate into a final reproducible text-analysis project; students with their own research corpora are encouraged to develop that work across the semester.

Text analysis as translation Representations are evaluated by what they make possible, what they preserve or obscure, and whether they are defensible for a substantive purpose.
Return to the evidence Source context, validation, and interpretive judgment remain central even when analyses use large-scale computational representations and models.
Build cumulatively Short assignments develop one piece at a time so the final project grows out of work students have already examined, documented, and revised.
Connect to students' research Students can use their own corpora so that learning the method also advances a real conference paper, article, dissertation chapter, or other project.
COURSE ARC

From corpus construction to defensible analysis.

1
Organize and preserve context

Corpus structure, units of analysis, provenance, cleaning, and sparse text representations.

2
Represent and explore

Weighting, similarity, nearest neighbors, dimensionality reduction, topic models, embeddings, and linguistic structure.

3
Measure and validate

Researcher-defined measures, supervised learning, predictive validation, measurement error, audit designs, and downstream analysis.

4
Synthesize and extend

Computer-assisted qualitative inquiry, geometric coding, large language models, and choosing tools appropriate to the research purpose.
