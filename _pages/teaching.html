Featured Courses

Computer-Assisted Text Analysis for Social Research

A graduate methods course I designed from scratch on using computational tools to study text systematically without giving up the responsibilities of reading, interpretation, validation, and judgment. Computer-assisted text analysis is introduced as a methodological workflow rather than a sequence of disconnected algorithms: students learn to organize corpora, construct and compare representations, explore and retrieve cases, develop and validate text-derived measures, use supervised learning and imperfect measurements, and integrate qualitative inquiry and large language models into defensible analyses.

The course uses backward design and a flipped-classroom structure. Short pre-class videos introduce core concepts, while class sessions focus on discussion, application, and guided practice. To make the technical work more accessible, students use TeAL rather than implementing common infrastructure from scratch, and incremental assignments build toward a final project structured around the methods and results of a substantive analysis.
